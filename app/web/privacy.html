Privacy policy

tosk, the free language-learning app · last updated 25 October 2026

In short: tosk works without an account, and then your progress stays on your device. There are no ads, no analytics and no tracking, and nothing is sold. If you sign in, your progress is kept in your own private space so it can sync between your devices. Reports reach the developer only when you send them, and Delete my cloud data removes everything stored in the cloud.

On your device

  • Your progress: every answer you give (see "What is stored per answer" below), your mistakes, starred items, AI conversations and your settings.
  • The courses you download, and the flags you submit about exercises (until you delete them).
  • Your AI key, if you add one: in the system's secure storage. In the web app it is kept in this browser's storage for this site.

This stays until you remove the app (in the web app: clear this site's data). Settings → Backup lets you export or import it yourself. On Android, if backup is switched on for your phone, Android's own backup may include the app's data in your Google account's device backup; you control that in Android's settings.

If you sign in (optional)

Sign-in uses your Google account through Google Firebase Authentication, which receives your name, email address and a user id for your account. tosk then keeps, linked to that user id, in Google Cloud Firestore (a database hosted by Google in the European Union, multi-region eur3):

  • Your answer log. Per answer: a random id, the word or grammar item and the exercise type, the time and your time zone offset, what you typed or chose, whether it was right, the rating the app gave it, how long you took, how you answered (typing, tapping, handwriting), the lesson session and practice mode, and a random id for the device it came from.
  • The settings that sync: your courses, translation language, lesson focus and length, accents, theme, sound effects, daily goal, answer input, listening exercises, the exercise intros you have seen and automatic course updates, each with the time it changed and the device id.
  • Reports you send (below).

Only you can read this data; the database rules don't let other users in. The developer can reach the database as its administrator and only looks at the reports you send. Starred items, AI conversations, your AI key, display, reminder and e-reader settings are not synced.

Reports you send

When you are signed in and you submit a flag, send feedback (Settings → Send feedback) or send a crash report, the developer receives it. A report contains what you wrote, for a flag the exercise and your answer, for a crash report the error details and your device model, plus the app version and the platform (for example Android or web). It is stored in your own space (linked to your user id) and contains your email address only if you type it into the feedback form. Reports can't be changed once sent. Without signing in, nothing is sent: you can export, copy or email it yourself.

Reports are used to fix exercises and bugs, and deleted when no longer needed.

AI features (optional)

Explanations, flag checks and conversation practice only work with your own API key (for example from Anthropic). Each request goes directly from your device to that provider: the exercise, your answer and, in a conversation, your messages. The provider's own privacy terms apply. The developer never sees these requests or your key.

Other services the app talks to

  • GitHub. The web app is hosted on GitHub Pages, and courses are downloaded from GitHub (GitHub Releases, or GitHub Pages for the web app). About once every 24 hours, when online, the app also asks GitHub for the list of courses to see whether an update is available (Settings → "Check for course updates automatically" turns this off). Like any website, GitHub receives your IP address with these requests.
  • Fonts. The web app may load extra fonts from Google (fonts.gstatic.com) when it has to show characters its own fonts don't include.
  • Handwriting (Android). Handwriting recognition uses Google ML Kit: the language model is downloaded from Google once and recognition then runs on the device. ML Kit may send Google basic usage and diagnostic information about the model.
  • Speech. Sentences are read aloud by your device's or browser's own text-to-speech voices. In the web app some browsers offer online voices (for example Google's voices in Chrome); if one of those is used, the browser sends the sentence to its maker to be spoken.
  • Sign-in. Google's sign-in (and, in the web app, Google's sign-in button) as described above.

Deleting your data

  • Cloud data: Settings → Account & sync → Delete my cloud data deletes your answers, settings, the reports you sent and your sign-in account. Google sometimes asks you to sign in once more before an account can be removed; the app tells you if that is needed. Your devices keep their own copy.
  • On a device: remove the app, or in the web app clear this site's data.
  • Copies of reports the developer already downloaded to fix something are deleted on request (contact below).
